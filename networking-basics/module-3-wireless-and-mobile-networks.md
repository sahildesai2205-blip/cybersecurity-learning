# Module 3: Wireless and Mobile Networks

## 1. Types of Wireless Networks

### Cellular Networks
Cellular networks use mobile network infrastructure and cell sites to provide services such as phone calls, messaging and mobile data.

### Bluetooth
Bluetooth is a short-range, low-power wireless technology commonly used to connect accessories such as headphones, speakers and smartwatches. It can transmit audio and data.

### Wi-Fi
Wi-Fi uses radio waves to connect devices to a wireless local area network (WLAN). A Wi-Fi network may provide access to the Internet through a router or access point.

### Near-Field Communication (NFC)
NFC allows compatible devices to exchange data over a very short distance using electromagnetic fields. A common use is contactless payment at a compatible terminal.

## 2. How Phones Use Different Technologies
- **Talk:** Cellular networks can carry voice and video calls; calls may also use Wi-Fi or Internet-based services.
- **Data:** Cellular data or Wi-Fi provides Internet connectivity for browsing and apps.
- **Location:** GPS helps a phone determine its geographical position.
- **Payments:** NFC can let a phone communicate with a compatible payment terminal. The payment service processes the transaction.

## 3. GPS — Global Positioning System
GPS uses signals from satellites to help a receiver calculate its location. Accuracy varies depending on factors such as signal conditions and the surrounding environment.

GPS provides positioning, not Internet access. Map apps may use GPS for location and Wi-Fi or cellular data to download maps and other information.

## 4. Quick Comparison
- **Cellular:** Mobile communication and data through a mobile operator's network.
- **Bluetooth:** Short-range connections for accessories and nearby devices.
- **Wi-Fi:** Wireless local network connectivity, often used for Internet access.
- **NFC:** Very short-range communication, often used for contactless payments.
- **GPS:** Determines geographical location using satellite signals.

## Key Takeaways
- Wireless technologies serve different purposes.
- Wi-Fi connects devices to a local wireless network; Internet access depends on the network's connection.
- Bluetooth is useful for nearby accessories.
- NFC enables very short-range communication.
- GPS provides location information rather than Internet access.
