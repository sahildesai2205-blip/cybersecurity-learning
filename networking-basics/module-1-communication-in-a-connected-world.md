# Module 1: Communication in a Connected World

## 1. The Internet
The Internet is a global system of interconnected networks. No single person or organization owns the entire Internet.

## 2. Types of Networks

### Home Network
Connects devices such as phones, laptops and smart TVs within a home.

### SOHO Network
SOHO means Small Office/Home Office. It connects devices so they can communicate and share resources, often with Internet access.

### Medium and Large Networks
Organizations can connect devices and smaller networks across offices or entire buildings.

### The Internet
The Internet is a network of interconnected networks.

## 3. Connected Home Devices
Examples include security cameras, smart TVs, washing machines, smart air conditioners, speakers and lights. These devices can communicate over a network and may support remote monitoring or control.

## 4. Other Connected Devices

### Smart Cars
Some cars connect to phones and the Internet for navigation, remote features and vehicle status information.

### RFID
RFID stands for Radio-Frequency Identification. It uses radio waves to identify tagged objects and can help track inventory or assets.

## 5. Data and Personal Information
Data is information that can be collected, stored, processed or transmitted.

- **Volunteered data:** Information deliberately provided by a person, such as a registration form.
- **Observed data:** Information collected from activities or devices, such as location data.
- **Inferred data:** Information deduced from other data, such as predicting shopping preferences from purchase history.

## 6. The Bit
A bit is a binary digit that can be 0 or 1. One byte contains 8 bits. Computers process information using binary data and convert it into forms people can understand, such as text, images and sound.

## 7. Methods of Data Transmission
Transmission media are the channels through which data travels from a source to a destination.

- **Copper cable:** Uses electrical signals.
- **Fiber-optic cable:** Uses pulses of light.
- **Wireless transmission:** Uses electromagnetic waves, including radio waves, microwaves and infrared, depending on the technology.

## 8. Bandwidth
Bandwidth describes a connection's data-carrying capacity. It is commonly measured in bits per second.

- **kbps:** kilobits per second
- **Mbps:** megabits per second
- **Gbps:** gigabits per second

## 9. Throughput
Throughput is the actual rate at which data is successfully transferred. It can be affected by congestion, interference and other network conditions.

## 10. Latency
Latency is the time taken for data to travel from one point to another. Lower latency is generally desirable for activities such as online gaming and video calls.

## Key Takeaways
- Networks allow devices to communicate and share data.
- The Internet connects networks around the world.
- Data can travel through copper cables, fiber-optic cables or wireless connections.
- Computers process information using binary digits.
- Bandwidth describes capacity, throughput describes actual transfer rate, and latency describes delay.
